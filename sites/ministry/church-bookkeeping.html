A lot of churches are one retirement away from a bookkeeping crisis.

Many congregations rely on one aging bookkeeper who's been doing it the same way for decades, often on paper, often as an unpaid labor of love. When that person retires or steps back, there's frequently no one trained to take over, and records can fall behind fast. Smaller communities often can't justify hiring a dedicated bookkeeper at all, so volunteers handle it in spare moments, which slows everything down. And when it comes to donor records and financial data, "whoever has time this week" isn't really a system.

FAQ

We collect cash and check donations in person: how does a remote bookkeeper handle that?

Nothing changes about how your offering is counted and deposited. That stays local, in person, and if your church already has two people count and verify the offering before it's deposited (standard practice at most churches, and a good internal control regardless of who does your books), that continues exactly as it is now.

The only new habit is depositing the full amount intact and keeping a simple record of what was deposited, rather than pulling cash out to cover a reimbursement on the spot; that one change is what makes remote reconciliation actually work. Checks are often eligible for mobile deposit through your bank, the same way many churches already do it; cash still needs someone physically local, same as today.

Our part starts once the money is deposited: we record and categorize it, reconcile it against your bank statement, match it to donor and pledge records, and make sure it's reflected accurately in your reports. The people you trust to physically handle the offering keep doing exactly that; we handle what happens to the numbers afterward.

Who actually sees our financial data?

Sayaka handles the sensitive parts of the work directly. Her supporting team handles routine data entry and transactions under her supervision; access is scoped, not wide open.

Can your team access our bank account or move money?

No. The bookkeeping team prepares everything: transactions coded, matched, and ready, but the ability to actually authorize a payment or touch your bank account stays stateside, with your church or a designated U.S.-based finance lead. Nobody overseas has that authority.

Do we have to switch software?

No. We start with whatever you're already using. If you'd like to modernize later, that's a separate conversation on your timeline.

What if our bookkeeper is retiring soon and we're worried about the gap?

This is one of the most common reasons churches reach out to us. Tell us your timeline and we'll build a transition plan around it.

Is this only for Family Federation communities?

This service grew out of the Family Federation community and this page is written with FFWPU churches in mind, but we work with churches and ministries broadly. If you found us from outside the community, you're welcome here.

Are you hiring our bookkeeper, or hiring your company?

Your church contracts with our business for the service; you're not managing an individual employee, payroll, or benefits. We handle staffing, training, and continuity on our end.
